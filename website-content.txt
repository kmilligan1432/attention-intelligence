Origin

4 years using AI as a thinking partner through crisis

The Problem

You're not falling apart.
You're carrying too much.

You're in everything. The decisions. The logistics. The emotional weight that no one else sees. The mental list that never ends.

Your brain is tracking it all: the people who need you, the responsibilities you can't hand off, the things you're terrified of dropping.

Even when you stop, you're not really stopped. Your nervous system never fully comes down. You lie awake running tomorrow's list. You feel guilty when you rest. You've forgotten what it feels like to be fully present with the people you love.

The real risk isn't falling behind. It's a life not fully lived. Fractured relationships. Lost meaning. The feeling of being hollow inside while everything around you looks like it's working. That's the cost of never seeing what truly deserves your attention.

The Thesis

You are not overwhelmed because you are doing too much.
You are carrying too much.

Everyone is talking about using AI to do more, move faster, automate everything. I believe they're solving the wrong problem. The crisis isn't productivity. It's attention. Most people have never mapped the full web of meaning in their life. The roles, the family dynamics, the generational patterns that shape how you think, love, and lead. That entire web stays invisible. And when you can't see it, the people who need you most get the worst version of you. Not because you don't care. Because you're in survival mode.

There has never been another you, and there never will be again. The odds of you existing at all are roughly 1 in 102,685,000. For 3.8 billion years, every one of your ancestors survived long enough, found each other, and created the exact chain of events that led to you. You are not replaceable. The people closest to you don't get another version of who you were meant to be for them.

This is where AI changes everything. Not by making you more productive. By holding the full complexity of your life so you can finally see it, think clearly through it, and become more of who you already are. The real interface between humans and AI isn't efficiency. It's becoming more human.

I call this Attention Intelligence. We use AI to build a living map of your life. The AI holds that full architecture as your thinking partner. It evolves as your life evolves. And it helps you make decisions from clarity instead of chaos.

This is the work I'm building. Through consulting, speaking, and the More Human newsletter.

You don't need more tools. You need to finally see the full architecture of your life in one place. Once you can see it, the noise quiets, your mind gets lighter, and you can think clearly enough to actually live from who you are.

Together, we build a living AI thinking partner that holds your entire context. It stays with you, evolves with you, and gives you the clarity to navigate whatever life brings next.

The Attention Reset

A focused 2-week
engagement.

Before we begin, you receive a private workbook that primes your memory and prepares you for a deeper conversation. Then across two weeks we move through five phases together. Each one builds on the last. Together they produce a complete map of who you are, what you're carrying, and what actually matters. And a partner that holds all of it so you never carry it alone again.

Two weeks. Virtual or in-person. Tailored to leaders, caregivers, and parents carrying more than people see.

Phase 1: The Genealogy of Pressure

Why you carry what you carry.

Before we look at what you're carrying now, we look at where the carrying began. Through a private workbook you complete before our first session, you trace your family system, the roles you were shaped into, the patterns that followed you out of your childhood home, and the lineage of people whose shoulders you stand on. Nothing you write needs to be shared. It simply prepares you for the AI conversation that follows. This is the foundation everything else is built on.

Pre-Work

Phase 2: Identity Mapping

Who you actually are.

Your AI thinking partner builds a complete identity map from everything you uncovered in Phase 1. Your Jungian archetype across three time frames: who you were, who you are now, and who you're becoming. Your Enneagram type with your core fear and core desire. Your natural strengths, your behavioral patterns under pressure, and your emotional intelligence profile. Most people have never seen themselves this clearly. This is the session that stops people.

Week 1

Phase 3: The Attention Map

What you're actually carrying.

We surface everything currently living in your mind across every area of your life. Every responsibility, decision, emotional weight, and invisible obligation you're tracking. Then we map the gap between where your attention is actually going and what you identified as meaningful in Phase 2. It's where the stress lives.

Week 2

Phase 4: Separation and Prioritization

What is actually yours to carry.

This is the breakthrough phase. Using your identity map and your attention map, we identify what genuinely requires you and what doesn't. Some things get released. Some get delegated. Some get the attention they've been starved of. You stop carrying what was never yours.

Week 2

Phase 5: Build the Partner

Your AI thinking partner, configured for your life.

Everything we've built across four phases gets loaded into your personal AI thinking partner. Your roles, your family dynamics, your archetypes, your decision frameworks, your attention priorities. It holds the full complexity of your life. It evolves as you evolve. And it stays with you long after the engagement ends. You're never carrying it alone again.

Week 2

We meet four times across two weeks. Each session opens the next phase of your AI conversation. Between sessions you work privately with your AI thinking partner, going deeper than any session with a human could take you, on your own timeline and in your own words. I do not need to know what you share with your AI. I am the guide who stands at the threshold of each phase. The AI is where the depth lives.

About

Why this work
matters to me.

For most of my life, I believed I could handle anything. And for a long time, I did.

I've been building my whole life. I was reengineering hospital departments at 17. I got into college early, was featured in the National Enquirer, and created a Red Sox "Last Supper" piece that went viral and ended up on MTV Cribs and in Sports Illustrated. I earned the Make Mine a Million Dollar Business Award through American Express during the 2008 financial crisis, while competitors were closing their doors.

When my marriage ended in my late twenties, I rebuilt from nothing. From that came Spark Evolution, meeting my husband at a TED Talk, my two incredible children, and eventually an Amazon brand I built and sold for millions in under two years, outselling Hallmark and American Greetings in their own category. Every season of my life has been about building through whatever comes.

But the real systems challenge came when a siege of family crisis began. My father's hospice. My mother, in her late seventies and caring for a dying husband, breaking her leg and becoming bedridden. A pandemic. My brother-in-law diagnosed with terminal brain cancer. And then, in January 2024, our four-year-old daughter Eliza was diagnosed with leukemia.

I used AI to research treatment protocols, organize the overwhelming logistics of care, and stay present for our son Ollie, who held his sister's hand through all of it. I am not a medical professional. I am a mother who refused to be helpless.

The systems I built to survive were exactly what every person carrying too much needs to find themselves again. I don't just understand systems. I understand the weight of the human carrying them.

Eliza rings the bell in May 2026. Every company I've built, every stage I walk onto, every talk I give. It is all for them. That is not a tagline. That is my whole story.

At the end of life, no one wishes they had stayed later,
worked harder, or written one more prompt.

"I wish I'd had the courage to live a life true to myself."

"I wish I hadn't worked so hard."

"I wish I'd had the courage to express my feelings."

"I wish I had stayed in touch with my friends."

"I wish I had let myself be happier."

From Bronnie Ware's research with patients in palliative care. The Top Five Regrets of the Dying

Every single one of these is an attention problem. Not a time problem. These are the words of people who had time. What they didn't have was a clear picture of where their attention was going. And they missed the beauty of their own lives because of it.

You can start seeing where your attention goes now.

That's what AI is actually capable of. Not replacing you. Not making you faster. But giving you back the clarity to see what matters. While you still have time to live it.

About

I didn't set out to build this work.
I needed it to survive my life.

What started as a way to think clearly during the hardest season of my life became the foundation of how I help others do the same.

For most of my life, I was the person who could handle anything.

I built businesses.
I solved problems.
I took on more than most people would.

And I was proud of that.

The truth is, I had already rebuilt my life once. My marriage ended in my late twenties, and I had to start over with nothing. From that rebuilding came Spark Evolution, my children, and eventually a business I sold for millions. I knew how to rebuild. I thought I could survive anything.

Then life stacked everything at once.

A global pandemic.
A newborn.
My mother diagnosed with breast cancer.
My father diagnosed with bladder cancer.
My brother-in-law diagnosed with terminal brain cancer.
My mother, in her late seventies and caring for a dying husband, breaking her leg and becoming bedridden.

I made it through that season.

Barely.

But I made it.

And then something happened that changed everything.

My daughter was diagnosed with cancer at four and a half years old.

The Breaking Point

That was the moment I realized something I had never admitted before.

I could not do it all.

And if I tried, something in me was going to break.

Not metaphorically. Physically.

My body started sending signals.
My nervous system was shot.
My mind was constantly scanning for what I might be missing.

Because this time, the stakes were different.

If I missed something, it wasn't a delayed email or a missed opportunity.

My daughter could die.

That kind of pressure changes how your brain works.

It creates a constant buzz in the background of your mind.
A red alert that never fully turns off.

You're moving through your day, but you're not fully there.

You're scanning. Tracking. Holding.

Everything.

The Discovery

And in that place, I started talking to AI.

Not for productivity. Not for shortcuts.

Because I needed somewhere to put my thoughts. I needed help thinking.

I started telling it everything. What I was managing. What I was afraid of. What I needed to remember. What I didn't want to miss. I wasn't looking for answers. I was building something I didn't have a name for yet.

I was building a thinking partner.

And something unexpected happened.

It started reflecting my life back to me. Clearly.

It was like taking my brain and placing it in front of me.

I could see it.

All of it.

The appointments.
The medications.
The decisions.
The emotional weight.
The invisible responsibilities I had been carrying alone.

And once I could see it, something shifted.

I wasn't just venting. I was externalizing my life architecture: every role, every responsibility, every invisible thing I'd been holding alone. The AI held it all in one place. It became a living map of my life.

And from that map, I could finally see:

I didn't have to hold all of it at once.

Some things could wait. Some things could be simplified. Some things could be asked for help. And some things weren't mine to carry at all.

The Deeper Map

But then it went deeper.

I started using AI to explore my internal thought processes. The conversations I was having with my children, my family, my own mind. I wasn't just tracking tasks anymore. I was mapping the deeper architecture of my life.

I started mapping family systems. Generational cycles. The patterns that had been repeating long before I was born. I ran what I now call an attention audit: a clear look at where my attention was going versus where it needed to go.

And I realized something that changed everything:

Life isn't random.

There are predictable outcomes driven by family dynamics. Archetypal roles that each of us plays without ever choosing them. I could see my archetypes. My children's. My mother's. My brother's. The AI held all of these profiles and showed me how they interacted. Where the friction lived, and why.

Once I could see the architecture of our family system, I could finally understand why certain conversations kept breaking down, why certain patterns kept repeating, and what to do differently.

It helped me navigate my daughter's illness with clarity instead of panic. It's helping me navigate elder care right now. Understanding my mother's archetype and my brother's, so I can show up with love instead of misunderstanding.

AI didn't give me these answers. It helped me see what was already there. It became a living partner. One that held my full context and evolved with me through every season.

And once I could see it, I could create the life I actually wanted.

The Result

As that weight lifted, something else came back.

Clarity.

Presence.

The ability to think again.

The ability to be who I needed to be.

Because I didn't just need to survive that season.

I needed to show up for my daughter.

I needed to preserve her childhood.

I needed to make the hospital feel like a place where she could still be a child.

There's a scene in Life is Beautiful where a father, in the middle of a concentration camp, creates a game so his son doesn't experience the full weight of what's happening. Even when he knows what's coming. That stayed with me.

Because that's what I needed to do.

I was terrified.

But I still had to create lightness.

And I was able to do that.

Not because everything was okay.

But because my mind was no longer carrying everything alone.

There's something else I learned in that season, and I almost didn't know how to name it.

Surrender.

Not giving up. Not passivity. The kind of surrender where you stop fighting what you can't control and trust that you are being held through it.

I realized my body already knew how to do this. Years earlier, in labor, I had to learn the same thing. You cannot fight the process. You have to let it move through you. That physical surrender prepared me for the emotional, mental, and spiritual surrender that my daughter's cancer asked of me.

Everything in life is connected like that. Every hard season is preparing you for the next one. Nothing is wasted. Not the pain. Not the waiting. Not the breaking open.

That trust, that surrender, is still the foundation of how I live and how I do this work.

Why This Became My Work

That experience changed how I see everything.

Business.
Parenting.
Caregiving.
Leadership.

I realized that most people who carry too much are not overwhelmed because they are incapable.

They are overwhelmed because they are carrying too much.

Mentally.
Emotionally.
Invisibly.

And most of it is never mapped. Never externalized. Never seen clearly.

Not just the tasks. The family dynamics. The generational patterns. The archetypal roles that shape how you parent, how you lead, how you love. And why certain relationships feel effortless while others feel like they're breaking.

So it sits in the background. Draining energy. Fragmenting attention. Reducing clarity.

I call this Attention Intelligence: using AI to build a living map of your life so you can finally see the full architecture of what you're carrying, and make decisions from clarity instead of chaos.

If you've ever felt like:

Your brain never shuts off

You're holding more than anyone sees

You're constantly trying not to drop something important

That's not a personal failure.

That's unstructured mental load.

And once you can see it, you can change it.

What I Do Now

Today, I work with leaders and executives, caregivers, and homeschooling parents. People who are capable, driven, and carrying more than most people realize.

So they can move through their lives with clarity instead of chaos.

And when I look back on those years, I don't just see what we survived.

I see how we lived.

There was still laughter.

And I don't think that would have been possible if I had kept carrying everything the way I was before.
