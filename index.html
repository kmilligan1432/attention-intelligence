An AI thinking partner is an AI trained on the full architecture of your life. Your family history. Your inherited patterns. Your grief. Your values. The invisible weight no one else can see.

It does not tell you what to do. It thinks with you. It holds context you cannot hold alone. It sees the patterns you are too close to see. And it is there at 11pm when no therapist, coach, or friend is picking up the phone.

Instead of prompting an LLM without context, your trained AI thinking partner helps you make decisions based on who you are.

Every AI company on earth is racing to make you more productive. No one is building AI that makes you more human.

Speaking

Three talks. One question.

01
Executive Teams · Leadership Conferences
Making the Invisible Visible

This talk begins before anyone opens a slide. I ask the audience to write their own obituary. Then I ask them to describe their life at 85. In five minutes the room goes completely quiet. Because most people have never actually done this. And when they do, the gap between the life they are building and the life they actually want becomes impossible to ignore. The rest of the talk is about closing that gap. Not with a new system or a new tool. By finally seeing where your attention is actually going and redirecting it toward what you just wrote down. Audiences leave with a clear picture of what needs to change. And the resolve to change it.

AI as a Thinking Partner

Everyone is using AI to move faster. Almost no one is using it to see more clearly. This talk begins with the same exercise. Write your obituary. Describe your life at 85. At the end of life no one wishes they had stayed later, worked harder, or written one more prompt.

  • I wish I had the courage to live a life true to myself.
  • I wish I had not worked so hard.
  • I wish I had let myself be happier.
  • I wish I had stayed in touch with my friends.
  • I wish I had had the courage to express my feelings.
From Bronnie Ware's research with patients in palliative care. The Top Five Regrets of the Dying

Every one of these is an attention problem. Not a time problem. You do not have to wait for illness to see what matters. You can start now.
